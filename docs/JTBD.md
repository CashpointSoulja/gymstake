# Jobs to be done

## My primary job statement

When my gym plans keep slipping, I want to make a clear weekly promise and see whether I am keeping it, so I can build a routine I trust.

## The situations I’m designing around

| Situation | Job I want to support | Evidence I would look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I’m planning the week | I want to choose an achievable target and understand what is at stake. | I can explain the target, deadline, and simulated loss before confirming. |
| I’m considering skipping today | I want to see my remaining visits and the cost of missing the week. | I use the dashboard to decide my next visit. |
| I’ve arrived at the gym | I want to check in with a clear explanation if location fails. | I can start a session or identify the permission, distance, or accuracy issue. |
| I’m leaving | I want to know whether my visit counted. | I understand verified minutes and the daily cap without guessing. |
| The week is over | I want a result I can reconcile with the rules. | I can explain my stake return or loss and my share of the pool. |
| My circumstances have changed | I want to change next week’s plan without rewriting this week. | I can identify the active commitment and the pending settings. |

## Emotional and social jobs

I want progress to feel calm and specific. I want a missed week to produce an understandable outcome without judgement. I want the leaderboard to offer context, while keeping the synthetic participants obvious.

## Jobs I do not claim to solve

I do not prescribe workouts, verify exercise intensity, replace a coach, or prove gym membership. I do not offer a real financial return in the prototype.

## My research questions

I will ask whether the stake feels motivating or stressful, whether 30 minutes fits normal visits, whether the one-visit-per-day rule feels fair, and whether users understand when next week’s choices become active. I will specifically test comprehension of the simulated wallet and the shared forfeiture pool.
